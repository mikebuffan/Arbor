# Annabelle engine acceptance contract

The suite is not complete merely because detectors exist.

## Chapter-level acceptance
For a canonical chapter, diagnostics must:
- flag density without banning ordinary words or devices;
- protect Gold / do-not-touch material;
- distinguish likely intentional repetition before recommending cuts;
- surface sensory defaults as an observation-expansion problem, not a thesaurus problem;
- treat archive humor and face-says-it shorthand as frequency-controlled;
- prefer human conversational behavior over perfect banter ladders;
- flag show-then-explain residue;
- flag generic body/touch shorthand;
- preserve close-third camera;
- test scene-state change;
- run a post-edit regression pass.

## Book-level acceptance
Across the manuscript, future corpus-aware aggregation must compare chapter-local frequency with whole-book frequency for sensory defaults, body reactions, jokes, rhetorical constructions, motifs and character-specific language.

## Chapter Two fixture
Expected review targets include:
- repeated coffee/wool/citrus/rain-family defaults when present in the canonical text;
- archive/evidence joke density;
- face-says-it shorthand;
- polished multi-person banter ladders;
- explanatory residue after behavior already carries the point;
- generic touch/body responses;
- Mercer power/fragility being named instead of behaviorally demonstrated.

The fixture must not require any of those targets to exist. A clean canonical chapter is allowed to pass. Diagnostics report evidence; they do not manufacture a failure.

## Still partial
Felt-Life remains partial until substantially broader state coverage and manuscript-grounded calibration are present. Character/relationship/timeline engines remain partial until they consume durable canonical state rather than regex-only text.
